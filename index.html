We checked two sets of records: BMI, your performing rights group (it pays songwriters when songs are played on radio, streamed or performed in public), and the MLC, the agency that collects US streaming “mechanical” songwriter royalties. A song needs to be right in both places for all of your songwriter money to find you. The MLC figures below cover the 109 recordings we reviewed.

  • You’re already a BMI songwriter, and you already have your own publishing company there, Howard Greer Music. BMI shows 130 songs under your name, and 61 of the 109 recordings we found are among them. You stay with BMI — there’s nothing new to join.

Section B · The gap, in plain English

What this means for your money

There are two different kinds of money in a song:

  • Artist / recording money — what Spotify, YouTube, and other services pay for the recording itself. If your music is up and being played, some of this should already be reaching you (or your distributor).
  • Songwriter money — a separate stream paid when the composition (the writing) is streamed, played on radio, or performed in public. In the US, performance royalties are collected by a performing rights organization (ASCAP, BMI, or SESAC), and streaming mechanical royalties are collected by the MLC. That money only finds you if your name and share are on the registration.
